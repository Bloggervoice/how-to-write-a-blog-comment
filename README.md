# How Do You Write a Blog Comment? (Examples, Templates and Tips for 2026)

Writing a blog comment sounds simple, but most comments never get published. Akismet, the most popular anti-spam plugin for WordPress, is active on **more than 5 million sites**, according to its [WordPress.org plugin page](https://wordpress.org/plugins/akismet/), and it automatically filters comments that look generic, promotional or automated. On top of that, WordPress adds nofollow to comment links by default, so comments written only for backlinks rarely pay off.

The good news is that a thoughtful comment still works. It can start conversations, build relationships with bloggers, bring readers to your own site and open doors to guest posts and collaborations.

In this guide, I'll show you how to write a comment on a blog step by step, with a simple formula, real how to write a comment examples, nice comments for blog posts you can adapt and the mistakes that get comments marked as spam.

## How Do You Write a Blog Comment?

**To write a blog comment, read the full post, mention a specific point, add your own insight or question, keep it to 2–5 sentences and use your real name.**

In short:

1. **Read the post** completely
2. **Pick one specific point** you found useful or interesting
3. **Add value** with your experience, a tip or a respectful counterpoint
4. **Ask a question** to start a conversation
5. **Keep it short, polite and professional**

## Why Does Comment Writing Still Matter in 2026?

**Good comment writing helps you build relationships, get noticed in your niche and bring real readers to your site, even though most comment links are nofollow.**

Benefits of writing thoughtful comments:

- **Relationships:** Bloggers notice and remember helpful commenters
- **Visibility:** Other readers see your name and may visit your profile
- **Learning:** Conversations in comments often teach you more than the post
- **Opportunities:** Good relationships can lead to guest posts, interviews and editorial links
- **Community:** Comments make blogs feel alive, which bloggers appreciate

Google recommends marking user-generated links like comments with "ugc" or "nofollow", which it treats as hints rather than strict rules, according to its guide on [qualifying outbound links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links). So the real value of blog commenting is relationships and visibility, not direct ranking power.

## How to Write a Comment on a Blog Step by Step

### Step 1: Choose the Right Blog and Post

**Comment on active, relevant blogs in your niche, ideally on recent posts.**

A recent post means the author is more likely to read and reply. A relevant blog means your comment fits naturally.

### Step 2: Read the Whole Post

**Read carefully so your comment shows you actually engaged with the content.**

Note one or two points that stood out, surprised you or connected with your own experience.

### Step 3: Start With a Specific Point

**Open by mentioning something specific from the post, not a generic compliment.**

- **Weak:** "Great post!"
- **Strong:** "Your tip about updating old posts with new FAQs was really useful."

### Step 4: Add Your Own Value

**Share a related experience, a quick tip, a result or a respectful different view.**

This is what turns a comment from "noise" into something the author and readers appreciate.

### Step 5: Ask a Thoughtful Question

**End with a question that invites the author or other readers to reply.**

"Do you think this works for new blogs too, or mainly for established ones?"

### Step 6: Keep It Short and Clear

**Aim for 2–5 sentences, about 40–100 words.**

Short enough to read quickly, long enough to add real value.

### Step 7: Use Your Real Name and a Professional Email

**Use your real name or a simple brand-style name, like "Sam from BloggerVoice", never a keyword.**

### Step 8: Put Your Link in the Website Field

**If the blog asks for a website, add your link there instead of inside the comment.**

### Step 9: Check Spelling and Tone

**Read your comment once before posting to fix typos and make sure it sounds friendly.**

### Step 10: Come Back and Reply

**If the author or other readers respond, reply to keep the conversation going.**

## What Is a Simple Formula for Writing a Comment?

**Use this formula: Specific point + Your insight + Question.**

1. **Specific point:** "I liked your tip about..."
2. **Your insight:** "When I tried this, I found..."
3. **Question:** "Have you noticed...?"

This simple structure works on almost any blog, in any niche.

## How to Write a Comment Example: Good vs Bad

**Good comments are specific and personal, while bad comments are generic and promotional.**

**Bad comment:**
> "Nice post, very informative. Thanks for sharing. Check out my website for SEO services."

**Why it fails:** It's generic, could be pasted anywhere and pushes a link.

**Good comment:**
> "Your point about refreshing old posts really stood out. I updated three older guides last month with new stats and FAQs, and two moved from page two to page one. Do you usually update the publish date too, or just the content?"

**Why it works:** It mentions a specific point, shares a real result and asks a question.

## What Are Some Nice Comments for Blog Posts?

**Nice comments for blog posts are warm, specific and add something useful. Here are examples you can adapt.**

### Nice Comments for Blog Posts About Tips or How-Tos

> "The step about checking search intent before writing was a game-changer for me. I used to write first and research later. Do you use any free tools to check intent quickly?"

> "I tried your third tip on my own blog yesterday and it already made my post easier to read. Thanks for explaining it so clearly."

### Nice Comments for Personal or Story Posts

> "Thank you for sharing this so honestly. I went through something similar when I started my first blog, and your advice about taking small steps really resonates."

> "I loved how you described your first year. It's encouraging to see that slow progress still adds up."

### Nice Comments for List Posts

> "Great list. I hadn't heard of number 6 before, so I'm going to try it this week. Have you found it works better for beginners or more experienced users?"

### Nice Comments for Data or Research Posts

> "The stat about mobile traffic surprised me. In my own blog, mobile visitors are even higher, close to 80%. Do you think that changes how we should design blog layouts?"

### Nice Comments That Add a Respectful Different View

> "I agree with most of this, but in my experience, shorter posts sometimes work better for quick questions. Do you think length matters less for answer-style posts?"

## Copy-Paste Templates for Writing a Comment

**Use these templates as a starting point, then personalize them.**

**Template 1: Specific + Experience + Question**
> "Your point about [specific idea] really stood out. When I [your experience], I found [result]. Have you noticed [related question]?"

**Template 2: Thank You + What You'll Try**
> "Thanks for explaining [topic] so clearly. I'm going to try [specific tip] on my [blog/project] this week. Do you have any advice for [beginner question]?"

**Template 3: Add a Tip**
> "Great post on [topic]. One thing that's also worked for me is [your tip], especially when [situation]. Curious if you've tried it too."

**Template 4: Respectful Disagreement**
> "I really liked your point about [idea]. In my experience, though, [different view], especially for [situation]. What do you think?"

Always replace the brackets with real details from the post. Templates should guide your comment, not replace thinking.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid When You Write a Comment?

**Avoid generic comments, keyword names, links in the comment body, copied text and overly promotional messages.**

- **Generic praise:** "Great post!" with nothing else
- **Keyword names:** Like "Best SEO Services" instead of your real name
- **Links in the comment:** These often trigger spam filters
- **Copy-pasting the same comment** on many blogs
- **Commenting on irrelevant blogs** just for a link
- **Being rude or overly negative**
- **Using automated commenting tools**, which look spammy and can get you blocked

If you've used automated tools in the past, this guide on how to [check spam backlinks](https://how-to-check-spam-backlinks.mystrikingly.com/) shows how to find and handle low-quality links pointing to your site.

## How Long Should a Blog Comment Be?

**Most good blog comments are 2–5 sentences, or about 40–100 words.**

- **Too short:** One-line comments look lazy or automated
- **Too long:** Very long comments can look like copied content or take over the conversation
- **Just right:** Enough to make a point, add value and ask a question
